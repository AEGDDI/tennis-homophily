  Part 2 — Olympic Cycle Descriptives

  Homophily shares are stable across 2018–23 (~40% same-nationality, ~53% same-language), then drop sharply in 2024–25 to ~31% and ~46%. This drop is a composition artifact
   — the 2024-25 GS data contains ~134 matches per tournament (vs. ~62 before), importing more early-round pairs with diverse nationalities. Winner teams have a +3.3 pp
  language advantage over loser teams.

  Same language is borderline significant for tiebreak wins (+20 pp, p≈0.11), suggesting language cohesion may matter under pressure — but it doesn't survive to 5%
  significance and language proximity has an offsetting negative coefficient (high collinearity between the two language measures).

  Part 5 — Olympic Interaction

  No evidence that homophily predicts better performance during the 2022-23 pre-Paris build-up. All interaction terms are small and insignificant (largest p-value = 0.57).
  Ranking and career win rate dominate throughout.
