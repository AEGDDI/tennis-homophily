Cultural Homophily in Tennis Doubles
Grand Slam Analysis 2018–2025

Prepared for supervisor review  ·  May 2026  ·  Code: code/analysis/homophily_analysis.ipynb

Key Findings

  • Cultural homophily does not significantly predict match wins after controlling for ranking quality and career performance. Same nationality (+3.1 pp, p = 0.23), same language (+3.4 pp, p = 0.70), and language proximity (+0.6 pp, p = 0.95) are all positive but statistically insignificant.
  • Same language shows a borderline pressure advantage: teams sharing an official language are approximately 20 pp more likely to win a tiebreak (p ≈ 0.11), consistent with communication benefits under stress — but not significant at the 5% level.
  • No Olympic build-up effect: homophily does not predict better outcomes during the 2022–23 Paris preparation period. All interaction terms are small and insignificant.
  • Homophily shares are stable pre-2024 (~40% same-nationality, ~53% same-language from 2018–23), then drop sharply in 2024–25 — an artifact of expanded draw sizes (134 vs. ~62 matches per Grand Slam).
  • Ranking and career win rate dominate match outcomes, as expected.

2.3 Comeback Wins

A comeback win is defined as the eventual match winner having lost the first set. This occurred in 395 matches (18.0%) of the full sample, and in 43.7% of three-set matches — meaning the team that won the first set subsequently lost the match roughly two-fifths of the time.

6. Discussion and Next Steps

What the data say

  • Language similarity has the most consistent positive sign across all three outcomes (match win, tiebreak, comeback), but never reaches conventional significance.
  • Same nationality is actually slightly negative for match win in the descriptor (losers slightly more same-nationality), and the regression coefficient, while positive, is imprecisely estimated.
  • Homophily shares do not rise in the run-up to the Olympics — if anything they fall in 2024–25, driven by draw expansion.

Limitations and extensions

  • Sample size: ~1,700 Grand Slam matches. Power is limited for detecting small homophily effects (≤ 3–5 pp).
  • Language collinearity: same language and language proximity are highly correlated; consider a single index.
  • Draw expansion 2024–25: the larger sample changes composition and may mask cycle-level patterns; restrict to comparable round ranges for a cleaner trend.
  • Conditioning on partnership formation: selection — same-country pairs may self-select into tournaments — is not yet addressed.
  • Pair fixed effects: a within-pair panel (same partners over time) would better isolate performance from selection.
